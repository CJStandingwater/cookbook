# Recipe Naming Guidelines

To keep the cookbook organized, readable, and diverse, recipe titles and filenames must follow the rules below.

## Names must be unique

Each recipe must have a unique title and filename.

If a similar recipe already exists, the name should identify what distinguishes the new version, such as its ingredients, preparation method, texture, flavor, or regional style.

## Names must contain 2–5 words

Recipe titles should be descriptive without becoming unnecessarily long.

- Minimum: 2 words — `Carrot Cake`
- Maximum: 5 words — `Creamy Spicy Chickpea Lentil Soup`

## Names must identify the type of food

The title must end with a word that clearly identifies the type of food or dish, such as:

`cookies`, `pasta`, `soup`, `smoothie`, `pizza`, `bread`, `salad`, `stew`, or `cake`

This makes the subject of the recipe immediately clear and keeps naming consistent throughout the cookbook.

## Names must include descriptive modifiers

The words preceding the food type should describe what distinguishes the recipe. These may refer to:

- **Texture or technique:** crispy, fluffy, roasted
- **Flavor or key ingredients:** garlic, lemon, chocolate
- **Origin or style:** Korean, Southern, Moroccan
- **Context or use:** weeknight, holiday, lunchbox

Use enough detail to distinguish the recipe without adding unnecessary words.

## Names must follow natural English word order

Recipe titles should use normal English adjective-and-noun order. A common pattern is:

`[Texture/Style] + [Ingredient/Origin] + [Food Type]`

Correct examples:

- `Chewy Chocolate Chip Cookies`
- `Spicy Korean Chicken Wings`
- `Toasted Pecan Banana Bread`

Incorrect examples:

- `Cookies Chocolate Chip`
- `Bread Banana Pecan Toasted`

As a simple test, the title should sound natural when completing the sentence:

> “This is a recipe for ______.”
